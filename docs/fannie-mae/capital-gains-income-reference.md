## What the section requires (paraphrased; thresholds verified)

- **Nature of the income.** Capital gains are generally one-time and are not part of stable
  monthly income by default. They may be used only when verified as below.
- **Documentation.** Signed personal federal income tax returns for the most recent **two years,
  including Form 1040 and Schedule D**, plus evidence the borrower **owns a portfolio of assets
  that can be sold** if more income is needed to make future payments.
- **Age of documents.** Current receipt of the income is not subject to the allowable-age policy,
  but the **asset ownership documentation is**: it must comply with **B1-1-03**.
- **History.** A minimum **two-year** history is required.
- **Continuance.** Not required to be verified unless the lender has reason to believe the income
  may not continue.
- **Determination of qualifying income.**
  - **Stable or increasing** trend: average the two most recent years of personal returns.
  - **Decreasing** trend: use the most recent year only.
  - **Losses.** Capital losses on Schedule D do not have to be considered when calculating income
    or liabilities, even if recurring.

## How the code applies it

- `computeCapitalGainsPath` qualifies income **only** from human-reviewed Schedule D totals for
  the two expected tax years and a reviewed brokerage statement with a positive market value; the
  borrower's declared monthly amount is excluded.
- Each annual amount is floored at zero (losses ignored), the trend is judged on the raw annual
  figures, and the average or the most recent year is divided by twelve.
- Anything missing returns `status: "unavailable"` with a `missingItems` list and
  `requiresManualReview: true`; nothing is estimated.
- Expected tax years are chosen conservatively from the note date (`expectedCapitalGainsTaxYears`):
  before the April 15 filing deadline the prior filing year remains the latest required return.

## Open readings for the founder

- The Guide does not define "portfolio of assets that can be sold" beyond ownership evidence; the
  code accepts a reviewed brokerage/investment statement for an investment-type URLA asset.
